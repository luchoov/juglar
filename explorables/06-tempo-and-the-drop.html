Tempo and the drop

Explorable 04 found that a drum was hit. A musician also knows when the next hit comes, keeps counting through a bar the drums leave out, and feels a build-up pulling toward its drop. This page is about how Juglar does the same, and what its director does with it. It mirrors crates/dsp/src/tempo.rs, the tempo and the beat's phase, and crates/director/src/feel.rs and schedule.rs, the tension and the drop. The theory is in note 11.

01Counting along: the autocorrelation

Below are sixteen seconds of drums, synthesized on this page; Listen plays them. Every 10.7 ms Juglar measures the spectral flux, how much energy just appeared (explorable 04). Minus its average over the last second, the flux is a train of bumps, one per hit. Now slide a copy of that train along itself: at a lag of one beat, bumps land on bumps; at half a beat, a kick lands on nothing. Multiplying the train with its slid copy and averaging, for every lag, gives the autocorrelation. Its peaks are the beat and its multiples.

Three details make it count right. Switch each off and see what breaks:

  • Spread each bump over three hops. A beat rarely lasts a whole number of hops: at 150 per minute it is 37.5. A bump one hop wide then lines up with the next one only every other beat, and twice the period wins. Set the tempo to 150 and turn it off.
  • Add half the value at twice the lag. A real beat lines up again one beat later; a lag that only catches the hi-hats between beats doesn't. This pushes the score toward the beat rather than its subdivisions.
  • Weight toward 120. Every tempo has a double and a half that line up too. Most music people tap along to sits within an octave of 120 per minute, so the weight picks among the peaks. It only picks: the peak it picks is then placed by the unweighted score, or a broad peak leans toward 120. On a minute of real metal, before that fix, the tempo crept from 134 to 118.

Try the breakbeat: at 174 it reads 87, half. That is the octave many listeners tap for that style too; nothing in the sound says which is right.

Optional, for the curious: picking the period (crates/dsp/src/tempo.rs) 
fn estimate(&mut self) {
    let power = self.correlation[0];
    if power <= 1e-9 {
        self.confidence = 0.0;
        return;
    }
    let c = &self.correlation;
    let comb = |l: usize| c[l] + 0.5 * c.get(2 * l).copied().unwrap_or(0.0);
    // The prior picks among the peaks...
    let Some(picked) = (self.shortest..=self.longest).max_by(|&a, &b| {
        (comb(a) * self.prior[a]).total_cmp(&(comb(b) * self.prior[b]))
    }) else {
        return;
    };
    // ...but doesn't move the one it picked: weighted, a broad peak
    // leans toward 120 per minute, and over a minute of real music the
    // tempo crept from 134 to 118.
    let best = (picked.saturating_sub(2).max(self.shortest)..=(picked + 2).min(self.longest))
        .max_by(|&a, &b| comb(a).total_cmp(&comb(b)))
        .unwrap_or(picked);
    // Between hops: the peak of a parabola through the best lag and
    // its neighbors.
    let (left, middle, right) = (comb(best - 1), comb(best), comb(best + 1));
    let curve = left - 2.0 * middle + right;
    let shift = if curve < 0.0 { (0.5 * (left - right) / curve).clamp(-0.5, 0.5) } else { 0.0 };
    let found = best as f32 + shift;
    self.confidence = (c[best] / power).clamp(0.0, 1.0);

    if self.period == 0.0 || (found / self.period - 1.0).abs() < SAME_PERIOD {
        self.period = if self.period == 0.0 { found } else { self.period + (found - self.period) * REFINE };
        self.challenger = None;
        return;
    }
    // A different period: it must keep winning.
    self.challenger = match self.challenger {
        Some((period, held)) if (found / period - 1.0).abs() < SAME_PERIOD => Some((found, held + ESTIMATE_SECONDS)),
        _ => Some((found, 0.0)),
    };
    if let Some((period, held)) = self.challenger
        && held >= SWITCH_SECONDS
    {
        // Keep the counter's position in the beat, so the phase doesn't
        // jump more than the new period makes it.
        self.period = period;
        self.challenger = None;
    }
}

02Where the beat falls

The period says how often; the beat also has a phase, where it falls. Juglar runs a counter at the period, going once round a circle per beat. Each hop's flux is added into one of 48 bins around the circle, wherever the counter is, and older additions fade over four seconds. Wherever in the beat the music hits, a bump grows in the ring, and that is where the beat is. The ring's center of mass (of the squared bins, so the beat outweighs the hi-hats between beats) says where; beat_phase is 0 there and rises to 1 just before the next beat.

Choose rock, every third bar with hi-hats only above and play: through the bar with no kick and no snare, the detector finds nothing, and the count carries on, on time. That is what lets Juglar land a change on a beat the music leaves out, or that the detector misses in a dense mix. Played through the speakers and captured like any music, a 100 per minute rock pattern and a 128 per minute four on the floor were counted at their tempo every second after the first few, with every detected beat within a tenth of a beat of the count.

03A build-up: tension

Dance music builds tension before a drop: for eight or sixteen bars a riser climbs, hi-hats get louder, a snare roll speeds up, and the kick and the bass line are held back. Then everything comes in at once. Here are thirty seconds of it at 128 per minute: four bars of groove, eight of build-up, four of drop.

Tension is the treble outgrowing the deep bass: the treble's one-second average above its six-second average, less however much the deep bass's does the same, scaled so that 0.06 is full. It rises over about two seconds and lets go in a third of one, when the bass comes in.

Untick deep bass only to use the analysis' bass level instead, everything up to 250 Hz, where a snare's body is: the snare roll speeding up then counts as bass coming in. Here the tension lets go when the roll speeds up, about seven seconds before the drop, just as it did in that first version played through the speakers. And look at the novelty: it crosses its mark during the build-up and stays high, so at the drop it has nothing left to jump with. The drop can't be one of novelty's moments; it needs its own.

Optional, for the curious: tension and punch (crates/director/src/feel.rs) 
let deep = s.spectrum[..DEEP_BANDS].iter().sum::<f32>() / DEEP_BANDS as f32;
let [deep_fast, deep_slow] = &mut self.deep;
follow(deep_fast, deep, BUILD_FAST);
follow(deep_slow, deep, BUILD_SLOW);
let middle = s.spectrum[MIDDLE_BANDS].iter().sum::<f32>() / MIDDLE_BANDS.len() as f32;
follow(&mut self.middle, middle, BUILD_SLOW);
self.feel.punch = (deep - self.deep[1] - 0.5 * (middle - self.middle).max(0.0)).clamp(-1.0, 1.0);
let building = (self.treble[0] - self.treble[1]) - (self.deep[0] - self.deep[1]).max(0.0);
let target = if self.feel.sounding { (building / BUILD_FULL).clamp(0.0, 1.0) } else { 0.0 };
let seconds = if target > self.feel.tension { TENSION_RISE } else { TENSION_FALL };
follow(&mut self.feel.tension, target, seconds);

04The drop, on its first kick

The drop is the deep bass hitting hard while the music is building up, or just as it lets go. Punch is how much harder the deep bass hits now than over the last six seconds. A snare has deep bass too (it is noise), but it jumps as much in the middle; a kick barely touches the middle. So half of the middle's jump is taken off. And since some build-ups keep a kick, the drop must also hit harder than 1.25 times the build-up's hardest hit from half a second to four seconds before.

With no kick in the build-up there is one drop, on the first kick of the real one: the snare roll's hits reach 0.13 here, the drop's first kick 0.66 (played through the speakers, 0.13 and 0.62). Untick take off the middle's jump and the snare roll's hits rise to 0.18: still under 0.3, but less margin. Choose a soft kick in the build-up and untick hit harder than the build-up's own hits: each of its kicks is a drop. Tick it again and only the real drop is.

Now choose a kick as hard as the drop's. The drop doesn't hit harder than the build-up did, and no drop is found: the change then waits for one of the ordinary moments after the tension lets go, and comes half a second to a beat late, as before tension existed. Even the soft kick is close: the analysis levels the spectrum to its loudest band, so with nothing else in the low end a quiet kick looks almost as big as a loud one (0.41 in the build-up against 0.49 at the drop). Build-ups that keep their kick are the rule's weak spot.

05What the director does with it

All of this feeds the director (crates/director), which decides how lively the picture is and when, to what and how it changes scene. It waits for a moment: the analysis' novelty jumping, the feel of the music (pulse, brightness, swell) shifting against six seconds earlier, or a drop. Each scene shows at least 30 s and at most 100 s; a drop may come after 15. A change lands on a beat, detected or counted. Then it listens for half a second to tell what kind of moment it was, except for a drop, which the build-up already announced. So a drop's change lands on its first beat; before tension, it came half a second to a beat late.

MomentTransition
a drop the build-up announcedbloom from the middle, 1.5 s
the music grew suddenlybloom, 2.5 s, pushed on by beats
it thinned outiris, the old scene closing, 4 s
it changed otherwise, or nothing happened for 100 sspiral, 3 s
anything, in quiet musicseep through soft patches, 6 s

During a build-up, the director changes nothing: other moments are part of it, and a change due by time waits for the drop, at most 30 s more. The new scene is the one whose character (how lively it looks, how much it moves with beats) is closest to the music's, avoiding the last four shown. In the calm modes, focus and relax, scenes stay for minutes and every change is a slow seep.

Optional, for the curious: the drop as a moment (crates/director/src/schedule.rs) 
// Compared with the build-up's punches from half a second ago and
// earlier: the last two slots may hold this same kick's attack.
let before = self.punches[2..].iter().copied().fold(0.0, f32::max);
let needed = DROP_PUNCH.max(DROP_OVER_BUILD * before);
let punched = feel.punch > needed && self.last_punch <= needed;
self.last_punch = feel.punch;
self.punch_slot += dt;
if self.punch_slot >= PUNCH_SLOT {
    self.punch_slot = 0.0;
    self.punches.rotate_right(1);
    self.punches[0] = 0.0;
}
self.punches[0] = self.punches[0].max(feel.punch);
if punched && self.since_tense <= DROP_WINDOW && feel.sounding {
    // The build-up already said what this is.
    self.remembered = Some((Moment::Drop, 0.0));
    self.listening = None;
} else if (jumped || shifted) && feel.sounding && self.listening.is_none() && !building {
    self.listening = Some((self.steady, 0.0));
}
